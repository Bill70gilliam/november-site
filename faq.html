FAA aircraft registration for non-US owners: questions and answers

November Aircraft Registration · Reno, Nevada

Can a non-US citizen register an aircraft in the United States?

Not in their own name. The FAA register is a register of owners, and the registered owner must be a US citizen or a US resident alien.

A non-US owner obtains an N-number by having legal title held by a qualifying US citizen or US-citizen entity, while the owner keeps the use and the benefit of the aircraft.

What are the ways a non-US owner can get an N-number?

The common route is an owner trust, also called a non-citizen trust, in which a US-citizen trustee holds title. Other routes are a voting trust for a US corporation with foreign shareholders and registration by an individual who is a US resident alien.

November Aircraft Registration uses a separate US company for each aircraft, with the voting interest held by a US citizen. The routes are compared on the page Alternatives to an owner trust.

How does November Aircraft Registration structure a registration?

Each aircraft is owned by its own US company, formed for that aircraft and holding nothing else. One company, one aircraft. William H. Gilliam, a US citizen, holds the voting interest and exercises it independently. The non-US owner holds the entire economic interest: the use, the benefit and the cost of the aircraft are the owner's alone.

How is that different from using a trust company?

A trust company is one trustee holding title to many aircraft for many owners. With one company per aircraft, the company that holds your aircraft holds nothing else, and no aircraft, money or records are shared with any other owner.

What happened to trust-registered aircraft in January 2026?

In January 2026 the FAA declared the registrations held through one UK-based trustee invalid, on the ground that the trustee did not meet the US citizenship requirement. About 800 general aviation aircraft were grounded. The company disputed the finding, and its trusts were later taken over by a US trust firm.

Owners who had paid their fees and done nothing wrong found themselves with aircraft they could not legally fly. The lesson for any owner is to ask, on the first day, who holds title and the vote, and whether that holder is a US citizen.

What should I ask any aircraft registration provider?

Ask who exactly will hold title to the aircraft and where that person or company was formed. Ask whether the people who own and control it are US citizens and whether they will say so in writing. Ask whether you will receive a copy of every document filed with the FAA about your aircraft. Ask who opens the FAA's letters about your aircraft and how soon you will hear about them. Ask what happens to your registration if the provider sells the business, retires or dies.

What happens to my registration if William Gilliam can no longer act?

Each company's governing documents provide for a successor, who is also a US citizen, to take over the voting interest. That provision was written in from the start, so that a registration does not depend on any one person being available.

Does a US registration reduce my taxes?

No. A US registration is not a tax plan. An N-number does not by itself change what you owe in your own country, and it does not excuse you from the rules of the country where the aircraft is kept and flown. Take your own tax and legal advice.

How much does it cost to register an aircraft with the FAA?

The FAA's own registration fee is five dollars. November Aircraft Registration's fee depends on the aircraft and the ownership arrangement, and is quoted for each aircraft on request.

How long does an FAA aircraft registration last?

An FAA Certificate of Aircraft Registration is valid for seven years, after which it must be renewed.
