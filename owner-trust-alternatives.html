Non-US owners · N-number routes

Alternatives to an owner trust for registering an aircraft in the United States

November Aircraft Registration · Reno, Nevada

An owner who is not a US citizen cannot put an aircraft on the US register in the owner's own name. The FAA register is a register of owners, and the registered owner must be a US citizen or a US resident alien. Most non-US owners are told that the answer is an owner trust. It is the common answer. It is not the only one.

Why owners are looking for alternatives

In an owner trust, a US-citizen trustee holds legal title and the aircraft is registered in the trustee's name. A trust company usually acts as trustee for many owners at once. The registration is then only as sound as that trustee's own standing with the FAA.

In January 2026 the FAA declared the registrations held through one UK-based trustee invalid, on the ground that the trustee did not meet the US citizenship requirement. About 800 general aviation aircraft were grounded. The owners had done nothing wrong. Their registrations failed because of a question about the party holding title for all of them.

How the one-company structure works

Each aircraft is owned by its own US company, formed for that aircraft and holding nothing else. William H. Gilliam, a US citizen, holds the voting interest and exercises it independently. The non-US owner holds the entire economic interest: the use, the benefit and the cost of the aircraft are the owner's alone.

No aircraft, money or records are shared with any other owner. Each company's governing documents name a successor, also a US citizen, to take over the voting interest if Mr. Gilliam can no longer act. He has registered aircraft for non-US owners since the late 1980s, and he explains the reasoning in his own words on the page Details & More.

Questions to ask whichever route you choose

  • Who exactly will hold title to my aircraft, and where was that person or company formed?
  • Are the people who own and control it US citizens, and will they say so in writing?
  • Will I receive a copy of every document filed with the FAA about my aircraft?
  • When the FAA writes about my aircraft, who opens the letter, and how soon do I hear about it?
  • What happens to my registration if the provider sells the business, retires or dies?

What none of these routes does

A US registration is not a tax plan. An N-number does not by itself change what you owe in your own country, and it does not excuse you from the rules of the country where the aircraft is kept and flown.

This page is general information, not legal or tax advice. Which route fits depends on the owner and the aircraft. More questions are answered in the FAQ.
